## Review notes

ColumbiaWalks 3.16.1 does not require an account, login, invitation, payment, subscription, special hardware, sample file, or activation step. Launching the app opens its five core tabs: Map, Report, Repeat, Saved, and Community.

Typical review flow:

1. On Map, tap anywhere near Columbia, Pennsylvania to place a report pin, or tap **Use my location**. Location permission is optional.
2. Open Report. Quick Report can be submitted without a category, location, or photo; Full Report exposes all issue categories and follow-up questions. Location and identification start collapsed. The screen has two explicit actions: **Submit complaint to CW** and **Submit to CW & Notify CBPD**. The second saves the CW report, then opens a prepared anonymous-tip draft that the user must review and submit on CBPD's official site. Selecting only **Crosswalk encroachment** or only **Missing sidewalk** reveals a collapsed, off-by-default test-email control. Its toggle becomes available only with a relevant photo and confirmed location within 5 km of Columbia Borough center; those items remain optional for the CW report. Adding a second Quick type removes the control. Version 3.16.1 sends an opted-in message only to a ColumbiaWalks-controlled test mailbox with `[TEST]` in the subject—not to Police, the Mayor, or Codes. The **Notify the Authorities** and **Page of Shame** links remain separate reporting paths.
3. Open Repeat. Sidewalk is ready by default. Take or choose a picture, optionally select **Missing sidewalk** and a lip height, and tap the submit button. A picture and location are required for Repeat itself. The hierarchy and selected subtype remain ready while the picture, comments, lip height, location, and per-event vehicle identifiers reset. Vehicle and seven other rapid hierarchies work the same way. **Crosswalk incursion** displays the same collapsed, off-by-default test-mailbox control and optional license-plate/state fields. Both camera and photo-library presentations can be cancelled without restarting the app.
4. Open Saved to view pending or submitted reports. For an authorized qualifying upload, the app separately displays report acceptance, server destination mode, and the field-test email status returned by the server (including queued, review, disabled, blocked with reason, deferred, completed, uncertain, or unavailable). Test mode is displayed as “ColumbiaWalks-controlled test mailbox”; missing or unexpected modes do not imply an official recipient. The app does not treat report acceptance or a completed send as guaranteed delivery. A pending report can be retried. Report details can be shared or deleted from this iPhone.
5. Open Community. **Prepare an Anonymous Police Tip** keeps a standalone draft local, requires a past/not-in-progress confirmation, copies labeled Subject and Message text, and warns that opening the official Police Department site is not submission. The Report handoff uses the same screen but clearly says the source CW report was saved and that ColumbiaWalks has not sent the tip or media to CBPD. It lists supported evidence formats and requires the user to attach files, attest, complete reCAPTCHA, and submit on that external site. Do not file a sample police tip during review.
6. From Community, open **Trash Cans**. Public Comment is the default, is locked to public property, and discloses moderation and possible publication. Private Complaint has a separate category set, can use public/private/unknown scope, and says it is not published or automatically forwarded to the Borough. Both require a written location; the confirmed report pin is optional. The client queues JSON and does not attach trash-can media.
7. From Community, open **Contact Us**. Robert is listed as the current ColumbiaWalks contact at **(717) 466-9069**, with Call and Text links. **Send app or project feedback** opens the anonymous-by-default private feedback flow. Contact fields are hidden unless the user enables them, and permission to contact the user is a separate consent toggle.

No account flows or purchases exist. The app does not display a public user-generated-content feed, so users cannot view, message, report, or block other users. Moderated public trash-can text may appear outside the app; raw comments, locations, and private complaints do not. Reports first upload to ColumbiaWalks. After a successful upload, only a report with the exact approved selection shape, an explicit test-email opt-in, `official_email_destination_authorized: "test"`, a photo, and a location within 5 km of Columbia Borough center enters field-test email processing. Standard Quick requires exactly one approved key; Repeat Vehicle crosswalk requires zero Quick keys; Repeat Sidewalk missing sidewalk requires one `missing_sidewalk` key. Mixed, duplicate, and hierarchy-stray keys are ineligible. Subject to delivery safeguards, the server may send a `[TEST]`-subject message only to a ColumbiaWalks-controlled test mailbox. Version 3.16.1 does not send these messages to Police, the Mayor, or Codes. Authorization does not confirm delivery. A Page of Shame picture may be published on the public website only after administrator approval. The app does not contact emergency services and directs urgent situations to 911.

Target audience and value: residents, visitors, pedestrians, and community advocates in Columbia, Pennsylvania use the app to document location-based pedestrian-safety conditions in a consistent format for community awareness and advocacy.

Core services and frameworks: Apple MapKit, Core Location, PhotosUI, UIPasteboard, and the iPhone camera; ColumbiaWalks HTTPS endpoints at `directus.rndtech.org/columbiawalks-api` for intersection lookup, report submission, feedback, and trash-can intake. The police-tip tool opens the official CRIMEWATCH URL externally and has no path that submits a tip or media to CBPD. The ColumbiaWalks server—not the iOS app—uses a ColumbiaWalks-managed Gmail account for the limited field-test email workflow. No Gmail credential, test-mailbox address, or official recipient address is embedded in the app. There are no ads, analytics SDKs, third-party login providers, payment services, or tracking SDKs.

Server-side weather context: after a report reaches ColumbiaWalks, the server may request model-derived estimated conditions from Open-Meteo using only incident coordinates rounded to two decimal places and the incident calendar date and hour. Open-Meteo does not receive the phone's live location, precise coordinates, narrative, photo, contact information, or report/submission ID. ColumbiaWalks may store the estimates and provider/query provenance with the report; Open-Meteo data is attributed under CC BY 4.0. The iOS app does not contact Open-Meteo, this adds no permission, and an Open-Meteo timeout, outage, or missing result never blocks report acceptance or storage.

Regional behavior: features are the same in every App Store region. The map defaults to Columbia, Pennsylvania, and the service is intended for reports about that community; there are no storefront-specific features, content, or payments.

Regulatory/content status: ColumbiaWalks is an independently operated community pedestrian-safety documentation tool, not a government, police, medical, legal, financial, or emergency service. It does not represent or act on behalf of a government entity. Version 3.16.1 performs no automatic sharing with local officials; its narrowly classified, explicitly opted-in email field test uses only a ColumbiaWalks-controlled mailbox. It does not provide regulated professional services or protected third-party material. No special documentation or credentials are required.
